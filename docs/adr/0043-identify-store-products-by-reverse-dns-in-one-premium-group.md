# Identify store products by reverse-DNS in one Premium subscription group

Every real-money product is registered in App Store Connect and Google Play under a reverse-DNS identifier built from the locked bundle identifier — `com.avk.stitchwish.coin_pack_300`, `com.avk.stitchwish.premium_monthly`, and so on — and that identifier is also the literal key of `COMMERCE_PRODUCT_CATALOG` and `PREMIUM_PRODUCT_CATALOG` in the backend, with no prefix stripping or normalisation anywhere in between. The three Premium Plans live in a single App Store subscription group, `Premium Membership`, ordered Annual (1), Monthly (2), Weekly (3). Family Sharing is off on all three.

The identifier scheme is forced by Apple: a product identifier is unique across the entire App Store and can never be reused once created, even after deletion, so an unqualified `coin_pack_300` is both likely to collide and permanently unrecoverable if it does. The choice to make that same string the catalog key rather than translating it is forced by `revenuecat-webhook.service.ts`, which resolves a grant by exact match on RevenueCat's `product_id`: any transform between store and catalog is a silent failure mode where a verified purchase produces no grant and no error. One subscription group is what keeps a player from holding two Premium Plans at once, which the Membership Period projection assumes; group levels make Weekly → Monthly → Annual an immediate upgrade rather than a deferred crossgrade. Family Sharing is off because ADR-0012's Commerce Transaction Binding permanently binds one verified transaction to one Registered Account and forbids transfer or merge — a shared subscription would emit entitlements for up to six Apple IDs, and every member after the first would be rejected as a binding conflict while the App Store still showed them as subscribed.

## Consequences

- Adding a product means creating it on both stores and adding the same string to the backend catalog. A mismatch is invisible until a real purchase fails to grant, so the store product list and the catalog are one artifact recorded in `docs/store-products.md`.
- Because introductory-offer eligibility is consumed per subscription group, a player who takes and cancels Weekly is no longer eligible for the Monthly Trial. This is Apple behaviour, not a rule the game enforces, and it is recorded in the `Monthly Trial` glossary entry.
- Enabling Family Sharing later is a one-way switch on Apple's side and would require replacing the one-transaction-one-account binding model, so it is an ADR-0012 amendment rather than a console toggle.
- Google Play accepts the same identifier strings, so the two stores stay on one namespace and RevenueCat needs no per-platform product mapping. The one exception is Play's base plans: a Play subscription is identified as `productId:basePlanId`, so the catalog key is recovered by truncating at the first colon before every lookup, on the server and in the client alike. That truncation is the only transform permitted between store and catalog, it is not configurable, and Apple identifiers pass through it unchanged.
- The reverse-DNS prefix embeds `com.avk.stitchwish`, which is locked. A bundle identifier change would strand every product identifier.
