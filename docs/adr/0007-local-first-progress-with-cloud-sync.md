# Keep stitching progress local-first and sync registered accounts

Every Stitch Action and Undo Action first appends a durable on-device Progress Operation containing a unique operation ID, device ID and sequence, cell ID, desired state, and observed base revision. Session Progress is the materialized view of those operations rather than an unversioned set of completed cells. Guest Player progress remains local-first and is scoped to one Guest Installation Identity, while its Stitch Coin balance, reward grants, spends, and Pattern Unlocks live in a server-authoritative Guest Ledger. Guest Data Promotion consumes that identity exactly once into a target Registered Account reached through signup or sign-in. The account's first Guest Economy Promotion adds remaining Coin, unions Unlocks, deduplicates reward and completion source keys, and transfers eligible pending rewards. Later Guest Data-Only Promotions merge session identities, local progress, completion history, and Pattern Likes but discard the Guest Ledger and all untransferred economic value. Overlapping sessions use Progress Merge, and each Like increments the public count only when the account–Pattern relationship did not already exist. A data-only promoted session for a non-free Official Pattern without an account-owned Unlock becomes Promotion-Locked: its progress and history remain, but local Offline Pattern Data is deleted and no artifact grant is available until the account unlocks the Pattern. Registered sessions then synchronize operations and checkpoints to the Game Backend asynchronously in batches while remaining fully playable offline. We accept an operation log, queue, anonymous backend identity, idempotent promotion, merge rules, and conflict-resolution burden instead of writing every cell tap synchronously, because interaction latency, offline play, economy integrity, and protection against app or network interruption are more important than immediate server consistency.

Progress Sync deduplicates operations by ID and performs a causal cell-level Progress Merge instead of replacing an entire active session with the last device to connect. A causally later operation applies its desired state, so an Undo based on an observed completed revision synchronizes normally. Only genuinely concurrent operations on the same cell resolve completed over incomplete; unrelated cells combine independently. Device watermarks permit acknowledged operations to compact into versioned checkpoints without losing a still-offline device's merge base. This deliberately favors preserving achieved progress over the last gesture only in a rare true concurrency conflict.

Session Completion closes the originating device's active session locally at one final revision and is later validated idempotently by the backend. The first accepted valid completion becomes the terminal server revision. Progress Operations uploaded afterward from another device are Late Progress Operations: the backend acknowledges them as superseded, the device adopts the completed snapshot and clears them after acknowledgement, and the UI may explain the cross-device conflict once. They never reopen the attempt, retry forever, or move automatically into Replay Session. Undo is free while the session is active, but further play after completion requires a new Replay Session with its own operation sequence. This accepts losing a rare stale Undo intent in exchange for permanent history and stable one-time reward eligibility; late completed-cell work is already represented by the terminal all-cells-complete snapshot.

Guest Data Promotion applies Promotion Session Merge when Guest and target account records reference the same Pattern. Two active sessions combine through Progress Merge into the target account's surviving active session, and the Guest identity becomes an auditable merged tombstone. If one session is completed and the other active, the completed attempt remains immutable history and the active attempt continues as a Replay Session. If both are completed, both history entries remain. Completion and First Completion Reward eligibility are deduplicated by target account and Pattern, preserving at most one active session without deleting legitimate play history.

Before Guest Promotion Preview, the device inventories local operations, completions, pending rewards, and Likes in a checksummed Guest Promotion Manifest. Pending rewards include their complete source, Session, Pattern, monotonic-event, and state-transition evidence, allowing the backend to validate each economic disposition before showing the preview. The preview binds that validation plus the manifest snapshot to Guest Ledger and target-account versions. When the player confirms, the client first durably persists Promotion Handoff, then sends the confirmed request; the backend acquires Promotion Commit Lock and the device uploads the matching immutable Promotion Transfer Package without Offline Pattern Data. Failed upload, stale state, cancellation, validation change, or lease expiry leaves the Guest session unchanged and requires a new preview. A serializable compare-and-swap commit resolves every previously disclosed economic disposition and consumes the Guest identity only after complete staging. Once committed, the target account is immediately usable and the promotion cannot be cancelled, rolled back, or returned to Guest mode. The backend processes only unresolved progress, completion-history, and Like items even if the app is deleted or the device is lost, while the local handoff reconciles status after restarts and deletes each source after an idempotent acknowledgement. Promotion-Locked artifact bytes are deleted after commit and before target-account gameplay, but their progress records remain staged. Unresolved items show Syncing with automatic and manual retry; repeated failure enters Promotion Needs Attention rather than expiring or dropping data.

A Registered Account's Stitching Session identity and synchronized progress may appear on another device, but Ready state is device-local. The second device runs Session Preparation against the existing backend session, downloads and verifies its own Offline Pattern Data, and completes the latest available Progress Merge before opening gameplay. This never creates a second backend session or changes the first device's readiness. If the backend session completed elsewhere, preparation adopts the terminal completed snapshot instead of opening active play. Guest Player sessions have no cross-device visibility before Guest Data Promotion.

Each Guest Installation Identity and Registered Account uses a separate OS-protected Local Identity Namespace. Sign-out revokes the current device session and locks that namespace but never silently deletes unsynchronized Progress Operations, drafts, pending Personal Patterns, or Offline Pattern Data; another signed-in identity cannot read or reuse them. Returning to the same account reopens and reconciles its namespace. Local Data Removal is a separately confirmed destructive action with an unsynchronized-data warning. Account Cloud State lives only in the Game Backend in the first release; Apple, Google, and email are authentication methods rather than independent iCloud, Drive, or email backups.

Gameplay Coin rewards also follow the local-first boundary. An online Daily Task or eligible first Pattern completion grants Stitch Coin idempotently to the Guest Ledger or Registered Account; an offline completion creates a Pending Coin Reward keyed by its reward source. The record survives Reward Day boundaries and is validated and granted by the backend exactly once when connectivity returns, so it is never spendable before reconciliation and a completed task or Pattern is neither lost nor rewarded twice. Stitch Coin spending and Pattern Unlock acquisition always require connectivity.

Connectivity State never replaces usable local content with a generic error. Ready Sessions, Personal Patterns, Stitch Actions, Undo, drafts, and local history remain available Offline or Reconnecting; each backend-only action exposes its own reason and retry. Failed or delayed sync, Processing Job, commerce, promotion, and moderation flows expose a privacy-safe Support Reference so a player can reach support without sending raw logs, artwork, credentials, or Pattern bytes.

An offline Save as New from the Personal Pattern Editor creates a Pending Personal Pattern with a client-generated UUID. It is immutable and immediately playable on that device, then synchronizes under the same identifier exactly once when connectivity returns. Catalog Submission stays unavailable until the backend confirms synchronization, preventing moderation from referencing content the server does not yet own.
