# Verify rewarded ads with AdMob server-side verification

Rewarded Ads use Google AdMob as the single first-release ad provider without a mediation layer. A verified completion means AdMob's server-side verification callback: after the player finishes the advertisement, AdMob calls the Game Backend with a signed payload, the backend validates the signature against AdMob's public keys, resolves the player from the callback's custom data, and grants 10 Stitch Coin from that Reward Day's Ad-Equivalent Coin Pool idempotently by the callback's unique transaction identifier. A client-reported completion alone never grants Coin; it may only update local UI while the authoritative grant waits for the callback. Duplicate or replayed callbacks reduce to the existing idempotent grant, and a callback that arrives after the pool is exhausted or the Daily Rewarded Ad Limit is reached grants nothing while consuming nothing, matching the existing pool rules.

Both Guest Installation Identities and Registered Accounts may earn through Rewarded Ads into their respective Guest Ledger or account balance. The custom-data field carries only the opaque backend player identity needed for the grant—never email, provider identifiers, or other personal data. Consent and privacy configuration (App Tracking Transparency, Google UMP consent where required) is operational App Store and Play compliance work, but the game's advertising surface remains limited to the explicit, player-started Rewarded Ad defined in the domain: no interstitials, no banners, and no ad-based AI Credit.

We accept a Google ad dependency, callback endpoint and key-rotation handling, a possible delay between the client-side completion event and the authoritative server grant, and forgone mediation revenue in exchange for a cryptographically verifiable completion signal that keeps the Coin economy server-authoritative, one SDK to operate at the three-ads-per-day first-release volume, and a provider whose rewarded format and verification flow are proven on both platforms. A mediation layer may be evaluated later from real fill-rate and eCPM evidence without changing the domain's pool and limit rules.
