# Require human catalog review after automated precheck

Every immutable Catalog Submission first passes through Catalog Precheck. OpenAI `omni-moderation-latest` analyzes its title, description, and Pattern Preview for safety signals. Perceptual hashing compares its Pattern Preview with existing and pending submissions and records identical or near-identical candidates as a Catalog Similarity Signal, including matches owned by the same player and edited resubmissions. Those safety and similarity results are auditable decision support: they cannot approve, reject, merge, or publish content. Deterministic invariants are different. Catalog Metadata Validation requires normalized non-empty text within limits, one supported source language, exactly one active category, at most five distinct active managed tag codes, and no malformed field. Catalog Technical Validation verifies the Pattern Artifact checksum, schema version, dimensions, palette, grid indices, decompression limits, and preview identity. Failure of either moves the submission into Review Quarantine and makes the backend acceptance command invalid; the moderator may only reject it as Technical Invalidity. An appeal may rerun validators to correct a validator defect, but the immutable snapshot cannot publish until both invariant sets pass. Once valid, an authorized human moderator reviews every submission with its metadata and Publication Rights Declaration and makes the contextual acceptance or rejection decision; only explicit acceptance creates the public Community Pattern and exposes its preview through the catalog CDN. Every rejection records one required Catalog Rejection Reason—Safety, Publication Rights, Duplicate or Spam, Technical Invalidity, or Quality Standard—and may include a moderator note; both are visible to the submitting player without altering the immutable submission.

After an initial rejection, the submitting player may create one Catalog Appeal for the exact same immutable snapshot. The appeal cannot edit the Pattern, metadata, or rights declaration and is assigned to a different moderator when operationally possible. Appeal acceptance publishes that snapshot as a Community Pattern only after current Catalog Metadata Validation and Catalog Technical Validation both succeed; an upheld rejection is final for that Catalog Submission. A player who changes any content must create a new Catalog Submission and pass through the full precheck and review flow again.

A Catalog Metadata Revision reuses the same human-controlled decision model for an existing Community Pattern without pretending that unchanged playable content changed. Only its proposed title and description pass the safety subset of Catalog Precheck, while its entire snapshot must pass Catalog Metadata Validation; Pattern Preview, similarity, and Pattern Artifact checks are not repeated. A human moderator accepts or rejects the immutable revision, but the backend cannot accept structurally invalid metadata. Every rejection exposes a Catalog Rejection Reason, and the owner may create one Catalog Metadata Appeal reviewed by a different moderator when operationally possible; appeal acceptance also requires a current validation pass. No automated check may publish the revision, and the currently accepted metadata remains public until human acceptance.

We accept moderation staffing, appeal queue latency, quarantined-content controls, third-party moderation dependency, perceptual-hash tuning, maintained metadata vocabularies and rejection taxonomy, and lower automatic throughput in exchange for preventing false-positive safety or similarity automation from being the final word, providing one bounded route to correct a human or validator error, surfacing likely copies without misclassifying legitimate edited versions, refusing malformed metadata and artifacts as backend invariants, applying quality and context judgments that automated checks cannot provide, giving players actionable rejection feedback, and guaranteeing that no player-created content reaches the Pattern Catalog without human approval. Safety and similarity remain decision support; metadata and technical validity are publication preconditions; none can autonomously publish content.
